Gmail does not have a "unsubscribe from everything" button. You unsubscribe one sender at a time, using a link Gmail shows on the message — or you filter mail you still need but do not want in the inbox.

This guide covers the safe steps, what not to do, and when a filter is the better move.

## How to unsubscribe on Gmail

1. Open Gmail and find a message from the sender.
2. Look next to the From address. If Gmail shows **Unsubscribe**, click it.
3. Confirm. Gmail sends a one-click request to the list. You do not need to open a browser form unless Gmail asks you to.

If there is no Unsubscribe link, scroll to the footer of the email. Legitimate newsletters include an unsubscribe URL. Use that. Do not reply to the message.

You can plan which senders to cut with the free [Gmail unsubscribe helper](/tools/gmail-unsubscribe). It never opens Gmail or clicks anything for you — it suggests keep / filter / unsubscribe and builds a `from:` search you paste yourself.

## What not to do

**Do not reply "stop" or "unsubscribe".** Those replies often bounce. Worse, they tell a sloppy list that the address is real.

**Do not forward the mail to a stranger "unsubscriber" service.** That hands a third party your inbox contents.

**Do not unsubscribe from invoices, receipts, or shipping.** You will want those later. Filter them instead.

## Filter mail you still need

For billing and receipts:

1. Open Gmail search and run `from:that-domain.com`.
2. Open any result → the three-dot menu → **Filter messages like these**.
3. Choose **Skip the Inbox** and **Apply the label** (for example, Receipts).
4. Create the filter.

The mail still arrives. It just stops interrupting the inbox.
